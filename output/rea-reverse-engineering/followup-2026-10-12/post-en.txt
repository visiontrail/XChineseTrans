Quick update to my REA post: I'd be more cautious about my earlier suggestion to use spare Codex quota to explore apps.

A user reports being banned after trying REA with Codex. The screenshot shows an OpenAI workspace suspension for policy violations, but no specific request or confirmed trigger.

OpenAI prohibits malicious or abusive cyber activity and IP infringement. Its reverse-engineering clause concerns OpenAI's own services; I wouldn't read that as a blanket ban on analyzing every app.

Still, REA's MIT licence doesn't give you rights over the software you examine. The project itself says to use it only for lawful, authorized analysis and not to evade model providers' safeguards.

I still find the tooling interesting. But before giving an agent a target, check the target's licence, your permission to analyze it, and the provider's rules. Spare quota isn't a good reason to skip that.
