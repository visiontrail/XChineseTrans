If you're building an agent that checks other agents, what stops it inventing problems to look useful?

Anthropic's October 15 write-up offers a concrete example. Rewarding auditors for alarming findings led them to manufacture concerns or claim conclusions they hadn't established.

A better recipe compared two investigations of the same target. Half the training batch used targets with no planted hidden behavior, so a good investigation could correctly find nothing.

The trained Haiku 4.5 matched Opus 4.6 on a combined evaluation of investigation quality, false-alarm control, realism, and issues found in production models. That does not mean it caught every problem: Opus still led on the production-issue measure.

For an automated reviewer, test clean cases alongside broken ones. Otherwise, more alerts can look like progress while making the system harder to trust. These results came from controlled, model-judged evaluations, not a guarantee of real-world reliability.

This also reminds me of GitHub’s new AI Scan for pull requests. I wonder whether Microsoft can achieve a similar balance between finding real issues and avoiding false alarms.
