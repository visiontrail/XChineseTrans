What happens when an agent system can generate work faster than people can review it?

Anthropic's new internal study offers a concrete example: by August, employee-directed agents logged about 6 times the estimated human work hours. That's a runtime comparison, not a 6x productivity claim. Human hours assume a 40-hour week; agent hours include inference and tool use, with waits longer than 30 minutes excluded.

Meanwhile, roughly 80% of elapsed time in interactive Claude sessions was spent waiting for human input. Employees increasingly built instructions, review loops and monitoring tools to manage the flow of work.

If you're building an agent system, that raises a practical question: which decisions genuinely need a person? A useful next step is to separate routine status updates from exceptions that need judgment, and give reviewers enough context to decide without rereading every run.

This is evidence from one rapidly growing AI company, so it shouldn't be treated as a forecast for every workplace.
