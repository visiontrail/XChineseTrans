## Primary-source checks

1. Bastani et al., PNAS (2025): https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
   - Randomized field experiment at one Turkish high school, nearly 1,000 students; classroom assignment, not individual randomization.
   - GPT Base practice improvement 48%; GPT Tutor 127%; relative to the no-AI comparison group, not percentage-point changes.
   - Subsequent unaided exam: GPT Base 17% below control; GPT Tutor statistically indistinguishable from control.
   - Results are short-term, on conceptually similar problems; they do not prove permanent deskilling or lasting transfer.
   - The journal flags an August 2025 correction. The correction landing page returned a cookie-required page during this task, so its content was not newly verified. The original project's report says it concerns production/affiliation information. This adaptation relies on the presently accessible main article and its unchanged results table, not on that unrefreshed correction description.
2. Kestin et al., Scientific Reports (2025): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6
   - Harvard undergraduate physics randomized crossover study. A structured AI tutor with expert solutions, questions, feedback, and sequencing improved immediate post-test scores relative to active classroom learning.
   - Immediate post-test evidence is not delayed retention or lasting transfer. We deliberately omit a broad claim that all AI tutoring beats teachers.
3. Bjork Learning and Forgetting Lab: https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/
   - Practice performance and learning can diverge; current fluency does not establish later accessibility.
4. Chen et al., Educational Psychology (2023): https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410.2023.2273762
   - The primary paper's accessible full-text search result reports a randomized worked-example experiment with 114 students. Worked examples benefited initial learning and near transfer. Used only for the bounded statement that an example can be a useful starting point for a novice.
5. Gonçalves et al., Educational Psychology Review (2025): https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10076-6
   - Retrieved the primary publisher entry; corrective feedback moderates retrieval practice. The post recommends checking and correcting errors; it does not promise that retrieval beats every other learning method.
