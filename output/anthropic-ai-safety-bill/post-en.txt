Anthropic is proposing AI safety rules—and tight limits on who can stop a model.

According to POLITICO, its draft would require independent evaluations and incident reporting. Blocking a model would require a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than $1 billion in damage or death or serious injury to more than 50 people, involving bioweapons, cyberattacks or a model escaping its developer’s control.

Commerce would have to seek a suspension from a federal judge in Washington, D.C.; other federal agencies would be barred from suspending the model. The proposal would also override some state requirements on disclosure, evaluation and incident reporting.

Clear rules and judicial oversight make sense. Can regulators move fast enough as new risks emerge, and does federal preemption leave gaps in state protections?
