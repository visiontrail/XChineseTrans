Cellular reprogramming is moving from mice to people—and the first ER-100 data are now out.

Life Biosciences, co-founded by David Sinclair, is testing whether three reprogramming factors can restore function in damaged retinal cells. Its FDA-cleared Phase 1 trial targets open-angle glaucoma and NAION, a condition caused by reduced blood flow to the optic nerve.

On October 8, the company reported that its first three glaucoma participants tolerated treatment through Day 56. Two showed preliminary improvements in visual-field testing.

It’s a tiny, open-label safety study. Human efficacy, long-term safety, and whole-body age reversal remain unproven.

The part I find compelling is trying to restore the cells’ function. Even a durable improvement in vision would matter enormously.
