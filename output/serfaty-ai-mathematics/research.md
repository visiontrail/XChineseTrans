## Verification and editorial decisions
French source text was recovered through FxTwitter and cross-checked with the live X posts. The live X interface automatically translated French; the source-language metadata and full French transcript were used for interpretation.
- Official lecture 6:49–6:57: Serfaty describes roughly 400 results and gives her own Fields-medal-level assessment. It is her evaluative judgment, not a verification of every manuscript.
- 22:31–24:34: problem solvers and theory builders; Grothendieck's yin/yang analogy; the future role is posed as a question.
- 30:55–32:47: craft, patience, and the time needed for ideas to mature.
- 33:02–35:41: mathematics as shared, transmitted, living human understanding.
- 36:35–36:46: Serfaty says some AI proofs exhibit striking beauty and humans do not have a monopoly on beauty. The supplied Chinese post's claim that aesthetic judgment is an established final human barrier is stronger than the lecture.
- 37:33–38:43: questions about new concepts, understanding proofs, learning from AI, and maintaining skills remain open.
- 38:56–39:10: curiosity and Grothendieck's reaction to a problem whose answer is already in a library book.
- Official OpenAI release: https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/ (October 6). It describes an internal frontier model, ongoing formalizations, and work to improve exposition.
- Current official revision history: https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md (checked October 15). It records three withdrawals on October 7, proof repairs, and 300/719 top-line results formalized at that update. Thus avoid presenting all announced manuscripts as flawless or independently settled.

The final copy uses the beauty observation and a conditional, personal view about making proofs understandable. It omits sweeping replacement claims, unverified numerical hype, and the Fields-medal comparison. Public copy contains no production notes or added hashtags.
